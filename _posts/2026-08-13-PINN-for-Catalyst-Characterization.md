## Background

Three-way catalysts (TWCs) are a key component of gasoline engine exhaust aftertreatment systems, enabling the simultaneous conversion of carbon monoxide (CO), unburned hydrocarbons (HC), and nitrogen oxides (NOx) into less harmful emissions. As emission regulations continue to become more stringent, accurate TWC modeling has gained significant importance for predicting catalyst performance and supporting emissions compliance.

The performance of a three-way catalyst is governed by a combination of exhaust gas transport, heat transfer, oxygen storage and release, and catalytic reactions occurring within the catalyst substrate. Therefore, catalyst models must capture the dynamic interaction between engine-out emissions, catalyst temperature, and oxygen storage state to predict pollutant conversion under transient operating conditions such as cold start and rich-lean excursions.

In automotive applications, TWC models are widely used for ECU control strategy development, model-based calibration, onboard diagnostics (OBD), and virtual validation. These models help reduce development effort, improve calibration efficiency, and accelerate the design of emission-compliant powertrain systems.
